## Plaintext (M2) vs TLS (M2.5), same machine

Compiled by hand on 2026-10-07 from the two generated tables above (medians of 3 runs each; same machine, same method).

| Case | Plaintext elapsed | TLS elapsed | Plaintext throughput | TLS throughput |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 × 1 MB | 11 ms | 10 ms | 91 MB/s | 102 MB/s |
| 1 × 10 MB | 25 ms | 44 ms | 399 MB/s | 228 MB/s |
| 1 × 100 MB | 216 ms | 373 ms | 462 MB/s | 268 MB/s |
| 10 × 1 MB | 26 ms | 39 ms | 380 MB/s | 258 MB/s |
| 20 × 1 MB | 53 ms | 66 ms | 374 MB/s | 305 MB/s |
| 40 × 1 MB | 90 ms | 128 ms | 444 MB/s | 312 MB/s |
| 40 × 10 MB | 619 ms | 945 ms | 647 MB/s | 423 MB/s |

Reading it honestly:
* Targets still pass by two orders of magnitude (1 MB single: 10 ms vs 2 s; 40 × 1 MB: 128 ms vs 10 s).
* TLS costs roughly 30–45 % of same-machine throughput. Server CPU stays ≤ ~115 % of one core; the **client process** (which
  decrypts and SHA-256-hashes for every simulated student) runs at 170–300 % CPU, so many-client rows are client-bound here.
  Real students each decrypt on their own device. Even the slowest TLS row (~230 MB/s single stream) is above what classroom
  Wi-Fi delivers in practice, so on real networks the radio, not TLS, should be the bottleneck — to be confirmed with `lan-bench`.
* Run-to-run variance is large on a laptop: one earlier TLS run (not recorded in the table) measured 40 × 10 MB at 4.06 s;
  the next runs measured 0.99 s and 0.95 s. Treat single numbers with care.
* Server peak RSS rose from ~190–225 MB to ~270–306 MB. About 75 MB is a one-time cost: the WASM memory OPAQUE's Argon2id uses
  while registering the class code (measured: 130 MB → 205 MB at server start). It is not per transfer; RSS stays flat
  across scenarios, i.e. TLS did not introduce buffering.
* Joins now take ~180 ms of client CPU each (OPAQUE with Argon2id). Here all 40 simulated students pair inside one process,
  so "all joined" is ~7.4 s; on real devices each student's stretching runs in parallel on their own CPU.

A one-chunk file can't benefit; 2–4 MB files save little teacher upload for ~1.5–2 s of coordination, so files ≤ **4 MB** go

### Reading the peer-assisted numbers honestly
* **Teacher bytes fall to 4–10 %** of teacher-only for 10–100 MB files at 60–100 clients, with every client verified and zero
  corrupt chunks. That is the metric that transfers to real classrooms.
* **Wall time is worse on loopback**: here the teacher is effectively infinitely fast and every peer's TLS + hashing shares one
  machine's 8 cores (peer CPU ≈ 400–500 %). In a real room the teacher's uplink/CPU is the bottleneck and each student brings its
  own CPU and radio. Whether wall time improves on real Wi-Fi depends on the network (single AP: shared airtime, see
  PEER_ASSISTED_MODE.md) and is **not yet measured**.
* **Variance on this laptop:** 60 × 100 MB peer-assisted measured anywhere from ~20 s to ~190 s across runs (the table holds the
  latest run). The slow runs show low CPU (waiting, not computing); at the time the machine had 12.7 GB of 13.3 GB swap in use
  (macOS `vm.swapusage`, 82 M swap-outs), and the peer phase's ~4 GB of worker memory plus 12 GB of page-cache churn pushes it
  into swap. Teacher bytes were 4–7 % in every run. Treat the wall times as unreliable; re-measure on a machine that isn't swapping.
* Simulator detail: each worker process hosts ~10 students, so it is given a larger libuv thread pool (real apps each have their
  own); with the default 4 threads the shared pool, not the network, dominated (60 × 100 MB: ~190 s vs ~20–37 s).

## Screen sharing — SIMULATED / LOCALHOST (Screen Sharing phase)

**Not Wi-Fi capacity.** One MacBook Pro (Apple M1 Pro, 8 cores): the real bundled LiveKit SFU, one synthetic presenter and
N headless viewers (`@livekit/rtc-node`, 10 per worker process) all on the same machine over its LAN IP. 1280×720 synthetic
screen (moving block + time/sequence stamps) at 15 fps, VP8 simulcast, 20 s per run. `node tools/sim/bench-media.ts`.

| viewers | joined | frames delivered | latency p50 / p95 (ms) | SFU CPU | SFU RSS MB | SFU in / out Mb/s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1/1 | 100% | 22 / 28 | 4% | 55 | 2.0 / 0.2 |
| 5 | 5/5 | 100% | 22 / 26 | 4% | 68 | 2.0 / 1.0 |
| 10 | 10/10 | 100% | 22 / 26 | 4% | 79 | 2.0 / 1.7 |
| 20 | 20/20 | 100% | 26 / 32 | 6% | 92 | 2.0 / 4.1 |
| 40 | 40/40 | 100% | 30 / 36 | 8% | 114 | 2.1 / 6.7 |

* **Presenter outbound = SFU inbound ≈ 2 Mb/s, flat** from 1 to 40 viewers: the presenter uploads once.
* **Host outbound is a lower bound.** Headless viewers send no render-size hints, so the SFU forwards the low simulcast
  layer (≈ 0.17 Mb/s each). A desktop viewer watching full-size gets the 720p/1080p layer, ≈ the presenter's ~2–3 Mb/s each:
  plan for **viewers × 2–3 Mb/s** of host outbound and Wi-Fi airtime (40 viewers ≈ 80–120 Mb/s — beyond one busy AP).
* Latency is capture → encode → SFU → decode on one machine, clocks shared; frames delivered counts distinct frames.
* CPU is the SFU process only (`ps`, % of one core); viewers' decode load is on the same machine and not counted.
* Not measured: real Wi-Fi, real screens, renderer-side decode cost on low-end laptops, 60 viewers.
